A view of the drawdown

1 year compares the latest available price with the highest reading in the past 365 days. The history high is the highest in available history; the provider may not supply every past trading day.

A deep drawdown can help you find something worth a closer look. It does not establish that an asset is cheap or a good investment. Consider the business or asset, valuation, risks and your own plan before deciding.

For stocks and crypto, peaks use daily highs, falling back to closes when a high is missing. ETFs use dividend-adjusted closing highs; the comparison price is put on the same adjusted scale while the displayed quote stays in USD. Days missing adjusted prices are excluded if adjusted history is available; a close-only fallback is labelled when adjusted history is absent. These ETF comparisons can differ from an intraday price chart.

Drawdown = (comparison price ÷ recorded high − 1) × 100. Periods end at the quote’s timestamp. A positive value means the latest comparison price is above the recorded high. Available history means all history the provider supplies, which may start after the asset’s inception.

Recent returns add context over the past 30 and 90 days. The recovery figure shows the percentage gain needed to regain the 1-year peak; losses and recovery gains are not symmetric.

GitHub fetches market data on an hourly schedule and publishes a snapshot. Scheduled runs can be delayed or skipped. Refresh loads the latest published snapshot; it does not request a new quote from Yahoo. Check the snapshot time and each card’s quote time.

Quotes may be delayed. Cached readings keep their original timestamps; a refresh does not make an old quote current. Cached rows and snapshots over two hours old are excluded from the review count and filter. Quote age can be longer when the market is closed.

Put it on your iPhone

  1. Open this address in Safari.
  2. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Turn on Open as Web App if shown, then tap Add.

Open the Drawdown icon whenever you want to check. No Mac is needed.

The app checks for a new snapshot on opening and hourly while open. GitHub can publish snapshots while this app is closed; iOS does not keep this web app running or send background alerts. Saved readings and your threshold stay in this browser on this device.
